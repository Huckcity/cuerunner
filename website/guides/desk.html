Scenes and the deskPro

Who is open, scene by scene, as a grid you can read from the back of the room — and a cue that moves the desk to match.

1. Tell Cuerunner about the desk

Cuerunner ▾ → Show Settings → Channels. Pick how the desk is reached — OSC, MIDI, or MIDI over TCP — and the address or port it answers on. The line under it reads back exactly what Cuerunner will send, so you can check it against the console's own documentation before anything is live.

Then list the channels — the strips on the console — and which DCA each one rides. Several channels can share a DCA, which is what a chorus master usually is, and the DCA rows read back who they are carrying.

2. Enter the cast

Cast is the people: a name, a part, the channel they are on and any note you need. Ensembles are groups you will open together — principals, chorus, band — so a scene can apply a whole section in one press instead of a row of them.

3. Build the scenes

Switch the window to Scenes, press Edit, and add one. Performers run down the side, scenes across the top; click a cell to open that mic in that scene. Done when you have finished.

Out of edit mode the grid is for running: press Recall on a scene and the desk moves. The column the desk is currently on is marked, so the grid always says where you are.

4. Put a scene in the cue list

Add a Scene cue (toolbar → More) and point it at a scene. GO now moves the desk as part of the show.

A recall sends only what changes: the difference between where the desk stands and where the scene says it should be. Mics that are already open are left alone, so a recall never bumps a live channel, and a scene cue is safe to fire twice.

A scene cue with no scene chosen does nothing rather than closing every mic on the desk. That is deliberate: the dangerous reading of an empty scene is "close everything", and it is never what anyone meant.
