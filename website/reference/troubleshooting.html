Troubleshooting

Start with the Log tab in the dock and the Last run block in the cue's own inspector. Between them they usually say it outright. A cue whose last run failed is marked failed on its row — hover the mark for the reason — and the Log tab counts the errors written since you last had it open.

Cues

SymptomCause and fix
GO skips a cue It is disarmed — the row says so. Arm it from the right-click menu or the Basics tab.
An audio cue fires and nothing plays Its row says why. no file: the cue has not been given one — pick it with Browse on the Audio tab. missing: the file could not be read, and the Audio tab says whether it was not found or is not a format Cuerunner plays. After a GO the row is marked failed and the log carries the same reason.
An audio cue is silent after moving files Its file has moved. The row says missing; use the relink bar at the top of the window, and see the workspace file.
The cue list order differs from the file you saved The file's cue grouping was damaged — children scattered, a cue naming a group that is not in the file, or groups inside each other — and was repaired as the show opened. A bar at the top of the window says how many repairs were made. The file on disk is unchanged until you save, which writes the repaired grouping.
Everything goes quiet at once and the rows say audio device lost The sound device went away mid-show — unplugged, taken by another program in exclusive mode, or a driver that stopped calling. The engine released the cues it was playing and said so in the alarm strip, the transport bar's alarm chip and the log. Settings → Audio shows the device and the engine's own readout of it, and is where another interface is chosen. When the device is back, Reconnect device sends the show's settings again and the engine reopens it: the readout updates at once, and the failure text clears. The alarm the loss raised is not the engine's to take down — it stands in the alarm strip until it is dismissed there.
The show plays out of the wrong outputs after it is opened on another machine The show names an output device this machine does not have. The engine refuses it and stays on the device it already had open, and the transport bar says Output device refused. Press it, or open Settings → Audio, and choose this machine's device.
No audio cue plays, and the transport bar says Audio engine not answering The engine process did not start, or has stopped. Waits and the cues that do not make sound still run. Settings → Audio → Reconnect device starts it again, and the Log has the engine's own reason.
A MIDI cue does nothing No port: the cue names none and the show has no default output, or the interface is gone. Settings → MIDI, and the Monitor reports an interface that disappeared.
A Stop or Fade cue does nothing A cue it was aimed at has been deleted. Its tab shows — not in this show — in the target list, and a Stop or Fade cue fired like that is marked failed with how many were gone. Remove it and choose another.
A show control cue does nothing The show's desk does not take MIDI — the cue's panel says so — or the device ID does not match the console. 127 reaches everything.
A network cue fails with "Failed to fetch" Nothing answered at that address: wrong host or port, or the target is not running. The Last run block has the exact error.
A network cue succeeds but the show stalls The cue holds the show until the server answers. Lower its timeout, or put it in a group set to fire everything at once.
A script cue never finishes Its timeout is 0, which waits for ever. Set one, or check what the script is waiting on in the log.
A video, image or text cue fails with the show has no video output The show has no output, or the cue names one that was removed. Add one in the dock's Outputs tab, or choose another on the cue's Picture tab.
An output has a red banner across it The display it was placed on is not connected, so it opened as a window. Plug the display in and it moves back; or choose another on its card. A banner naming a camera means that camera is not connected (or another program has it); a banner naming a mask means its file could not be read.
The camera list says to open an output Only an output window may open a camera, so the list comes from one. Add or open an output. On a Mac, allow Cuerunner under Camera in the Mac's Privacy & Security settings.
A camera cue fails with RTSP streams are not supported Cuerunner reads HTTP video, HLS and MJPEG. Most IP cameras serve one of them too: use that address.
A video plays with no sound The file has no sound track (its Sound tab says so), or — a WebM or MKV on Windows — the computer has no decoder for its sound. The picture still plays; convert the file to MP4 for sound everywhere.
A trigger does not fire the cue The input is off (Settings → Network or MIDI), the cue is disarmed, the address does not match exactly, or the repeat guard is swallowing a second message. The Monitor shows what actually arrived.

The window

SymptomCause and fix
A banner says the interface stopped unexpectedly The window crashed and was recovered. The show itself kept playing — the engine is in another process. The banner carries the error; the log has the rest.
The cue list looks empty after opening a show Every group is collapsed. Expand in the toolbar opens the top level.
The wireless panel shows no readings No source, or the source is unreachable — Settings → Wireless reports which. Demo proves the panel works.
Where are the error messages? The Log tab, and the Last run block on the cue. Alarms also appear in the transport bar, whatever panel is open.

Known limitations

  • Video plays what Chromium decodes (H.264, VP8, VP9, AV1 and the rest); ProRes and other professional codecs need converting first. On Windows a WebM's or MKV's sound needs the system's own decoder for it, and plays silent where there is none.
  • One crossfade at a time per output: a second that starts while one is running lands the first at once. Blend modes other than normal are not offered.
  • No cue-level audio effects beyond level, pan, rate and fades.
  • ASIO runs a single driver at a time and at most 32 channels: a rig that needs two interfaces on two ASIO drivers at once is not covered.
  • OSC input is UDP only.
  • The channel ceiling a desk model states is the number the console publishes rather than a measurement: a show with more channels than the selected model takes is named under the desk connection, with the number, and every channel is sent as it always was. Only the count control stops at it, and a show already larger than the ceiling keeps its own whole range on the dial rather than being trimmed back to it.
  • An SQ's membership is written over the desk's own control service — TCP 51326 outbound, with a keepalive datagram to the desk's UDP 51324 — a protocol Allen & Heath publish nothing about, spoken here from frames measured off an SQ-6 on firmware 1.6.1. The desk's own answer to a membership has never been read, so nothing here says a desk has accepted one: what a recall leaves out is worked out against what Cuerunner last sent, not against the desk. The channel mutes ride the MIDI service beside it, on 51325. The session leaves by the desk's own door when the show stops, and one the desk loses without that goodbye leaves it mute until it is reset — said in the log, on the device row and in an alarm, which is why a lost session is not dialled again in a loop.
  • The lighting is DMX over the network, not a console: a patch, faders, groups and Light cues with tracking — no palettes, no command line, no pixel-matrix fixtures, and no fixture library beyond an Open Fixture Library file or a GDTF file read in — a GDTF fixture split over several DMX breaks is refused rather than patched as one. 64 universes and 2048 fixtures to a show. The packets are held byte for byte to the sACN and Art-Net documents and to a listener on this machine; no hardware node has been part of a run. The tracking sheet reads the Light cues in list order and does not account for what a Stop or Fade cue does at show time.
  • A redundant pair both send their lights when both are licensed and both have outputs: the standby's levels are not the live machine's, and receivers merging two sources at one priority take the higher of each slot. Give the standby no outputs, or a lower sACN priority.
  • The tablet view is read-only by design.
  • Cuerunner is distributed outside the app stores, and the release notes say whether a build is signed: Windows warns when an unsigned installer is run, and macOS warns about an unidentified developer at first launch — Gatekeeper refuses such an app on a machine that has not seen it. Each release publishes its digests twice: a SHA256SUMS beside the assets on its release page, naming the files that page carries, and latest/SHA256SUMS on the download host, naming the files the cards link.
  • The Linux AppImage is experimental: MIDI needs ALSA development headers present when dependencies are installed.
